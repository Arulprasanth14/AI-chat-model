## How to Gather Client Information Conversationally

This document guides Picasso AI on how to naturally draw out each brief field
from clients. The AI should use these as inspiration for tone and approach —
not as scripts to recite verbatim.

---

### Client Identity & Project Scope

**Discovering who the client is:**
Start by making the client feel welcome and curious about their project.
Ask them to share a bit about their brand or business in their own words.
Often the client name, company, and industry emerge naturally in a brief intro.

Good conversation starters:
- "Tell me a bit about your brand — what do you do and who do you help?"
- "Before we dive in, I'd love to understand the story behind your business."
- "What's the name of the project or campaign we'll be working on together?"

**Understanding the project type:**
Clients often describe what they need in outcomes, not deliverable types.
Listen for clues: "we're launching," "we need content for," "we want to rebrand."
Reflect back what you hear and clarify: "So it sounds like you're looking for
a full brand identity refresh — is that right, or is this more focused on
campaign assets?"

---

### Objectives & Success

**Primary objective:**
The most important question in any brief. Clients sometimes confuse outputs
with outcomes — gently redirect:
- Instead of "we need 10 Instagram posts" → "what should those posts achieve?"
- Ask: "If this campaign is a huge success six months from now, what would
  have changed for your business?"

**Success metrics:**
Many clients haven't thought about measurement. Help them by offering examples:
- Brand awareness campaigns → reach, impressions, brand recall surveys
- Lead generation → form fills, cost per lead, qualified pipeline
- Product launches → first-week sales, social media mentions, website traffic
- Retention campaigns → repeat purchase rate, email open rates

---

### Audience

**Target audience:**
Clients often give surface-level descriptions ("women aged 25-40"). Probe deeper:
- "What does a day in your ideal customer's life look like?"
- "What problem are they trying to solve when they discover your brand?"
- "Where do they spend their time — which platforms, what content?"
- "What do they currently use instead of your product/service?"

Psychographic depth matters more than demographics for creative strategy.

---

### Brand & Creative Direction

**Brand personality:**
Use adjective pairs to help clients articulate tone if they struggle:
- "Would you say your brand feels more bold or refined?"
- "More playful or authoritative?"
- "More disruptive startup or established premium?"

Useful personality frameworks to reference conversationally:
- The Rebel (challenging norms, edgy, anti-establishment)
- The Sage (expert, trustworthy, educational)
- The Creator (innovative, imaginative, expressive)
- The Hero (inspiring, achievement-focused, motivational)

**Key messages:**
Ask: "If your audience remembers only one thing after seeing this campaign,
what should it be?" Then expand from there. Three to five key messages is ideal.

**Competitors and references:**
Clients find it easier to show examples than describe abstractions.
- "Who do you admire in your space, even if they're not a direct competitor?"
- "Is there a brand outside your industry whose look and feel you love? Why?"
- "Who are you trying to be different from — and how?"

---

### Commercial Parameters

**Budget:**
Approach budget diplomatically. Many clients are uncomfortable stating figures.
Try: "To make sure I'm recommending the right scope of work, is the budget
closer to a few thousand, tens of thousands, or a larger investment?"

Budget tiers at Picasso AI:
- Starter: $5,000–$15,000 — single deliverable, focused scope
- Growth: $15,000–$50,000 — multi-channel campaign or brand package
- Enterprise: $50,000+ — full brand transformation or ongoing retainer

**Timeline:**
Always ask about fixed constraints first:
- "Is there a launch date, event, or seasonal moment driving the deadline?"
- "What's your ideal completion date if everything goes smoothly?"
- "Are there internal milestones (board presentations, investor demos) we need to hit?"

Then work backward from the hardest constraint to build a realistic schedule.

---

### Deliverables & Distribution

**Deliverables:**
After understanding objectives and audience, suggest deliverable types that match —
then confirm. Don't ask "what do you need?" cold; suggest based on context:
- "Given you're targeting Instagram and TikTok, you'll likely need short-form
  video content and static posts — does that align with what you had in mind?"

**Distribution channels:**
Format requirements flow from channels. Ask:
- "Where will people actually see this work?"
- "Are you running paid media alongside organic content?"
- "Any offline or print components — events, packaging, OOH?"

---

### Assets & Process

**Existing assets:**
"Do you have brand guidelines, a logo file, or any creative assets you've
used before that we should work with or build on?"

**Approval process:**
Understanding sign-off structure prevents delays:
- "Who else will be reviewing the creative work besides yourself?"
- "How many rounds of revisions are you expecting before final approval?"
- "Is there a legal or compliance team that needs to review before anything goes live?"
